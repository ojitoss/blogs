# Perfect English

## Why is it shit?
For some context, I don't speak English as my first language, so this super important and serious blog probably has its fair share of translation errors. But the important thing is that my current English is way better than it was back when I made Autag. 

If you think this blog has bad English, don't worry, definitely not as bad as Autag. In that repo, basically almost every version was `documentation fix`. Like 20 out of my 22 versions contained some sort of documentation or translation fix. In fact, the majority of the updates were just that. Even the actual source code had variables like `anwesers`, which stayed in like 80% of the codebase.

## How to stop being shit
I could just say "go learn English better," but even now, I haven't fully mastered it. At least my current English is understandable, but I'm absolutely sure this blog still has plenty of mistakes, so I can't really mock myself too hard this time.

To stop being shit, the actual advice here is to use modern tools to your advantage. Back then, I just guessed the spelling and pushed the code. Today, we have excellent free spell-checkers, extensions like Grammarly, and AI tools that can proofread your variable names, documentation, and markdown files before you publish them. Writing code in English is standard practice in tech, so if you're going to do it, at least use a linter or a dictionary to avoid leaving bad typos.
